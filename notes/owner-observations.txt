OWNER OBSERVATIONS -- what the person who owns the machine reported
===================================================================

These are not measurements made by this session. They are observations made by
the owner of the disc image, on his own equipment, reported to the session and
recorded here verbatim in substance. Nothing on the disc was executed by the
session itself.

Asked at the START of the session, before the predictions were finished, as the
brief required.


1. THE AUDIO -- ASKED, ANSWERED, AND THE ANSWER SETTLES THE THESIS CHAPTER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Question: the 22:30.04 of uncompressed AIFF -- 19 files in /data/sounds/music
named Atmosphere I..III, Scrolling I..V, Combat I..III, Nightmare, Fanfare I/II,
End Game, End, Intro II, Lion -- is it music or is it narration? The difference
decides whether the thesis chapter is about a soundtrack or about a talking
dungeon.

ANSWER, from the owner, after listening:

  "Gli aiff in music sono tutti musica di sottofondo, correlata al titolo
   (musiche di atmosfera/ambience, combattimento, fanfara ecc.)"

  -- the AIFF files in /music are all background music, and each matches its
  own filename: atmosphere/ambience pieces, combat pieces, a fanfare, and so on.

CONSEQUENCE: the 209 MB is a SOUNDTRACK. There is no narration in it. The
thesis chapter is about a studio that spent three quarters of a CD on
background music and did not compress it.


2. THE TEN NAMED SOUND EFFECTS -- CONFIRMED AGAINST THEIR FILENAMES
-------------------------------------------------------------------

The owner listened to the ten .aiff files that sit directly in /data/sounds:

    Buzzer.aiff          Click.aiff           DoorClose.aiff
    DoorOpen.aiff        Impact.aiff          Launch.aiff
    Player female hit.aiff                    Player male hit.aiff
    Spell.aiff           Swish.aiff

ANSWER: "sono letteralmente suoni indicati dai propri nomi" -- they are
literally the sounds their own names describe.

CONSEQUENCE: ten of ten filenames are truthful about their contents. This is
the same check the previous objects got on twelve WAV files and on seven
PC-speaker tables, and it is the only thing in this repository that connects a
byte count to a sound. It is an OBSERVATION, attributed, not a measurement.

5. ONE GROWL IS A STOCK RECORDING, AND THE OWNER RECOGNISED IT
---------------------------------------------------------------

Unprompted, on /data/sounds/growls/Ankheg.aiff:

  "e' il famoso 'ruggito da T-Rex' (non quello di Jurassic Park, quello di
   Land Before Time e altri T-Rex/demoni dell'epoca)"

  -- it is the well-known stock tyrannosaur roar of the period, the one heard
  in The Land Before Time and in other early-1990s dinosaurs and demons, not
  the Jurassic Park one.

CONSEQUENCE, and it is a hypothesis this session cannot test: at least one of
the forty-three creature voices is a LIBRARY recording rather than an original
one. The session cannot verify a sound-effects library from bytes -- there is
nothing on this disc to compare it against, and no external audio is admitted.
It is recorded as an attributed observation and it is NOT promoted to a claim.

What it does earn is a sharper reading of finding in the growls chapter: eight
creatures sharing four voices byte for byte is a re-use INSIDE the disc, and
this is a report of re-use from OUTSIDE it. The two are different facts and
only the first one is measured here.

6. AND HE NAMED THE LIBRARY ENTRY
----------------------------------

Follow-up, unprompted, on the same file: the owner identified it as the
Sound Ideas library cue

    "Sound Ideas, ANIMAL, CREATURE -- LARGE ANIMAL ROAR 01"

and gave a URL on a sound-effects wiki. The owner then clarified that this
pipeline's "no network" rule is about a product's own online services and not
about looking something up, so the page WAS requested. **It returned HTTP 402
and its body was never retrieved**, so the identification still rests on the
owner's ear and on nothing this session read.

It is recorded here for exactly what it is: a named hypothesis, from a person,
about one of the forty-three creature voices, which this repository does not
test and does not publish as a finding.

The owner then supplied the page's contents by hand. What it says, quoted as
his relay and not as anything this session read from the web:

  * the cue is from **The General Series 6000 Sound Effects Library**, by
    Sound Ideas;
  * it is the same track as "MONSTER, FANTASY -- LARGE CREATURE: SCREECHING
    ROAR 01";
  * its earliest listed use is the 1985 film The Black Cauldron, and it is the
    roar in The Land Before Time (1988);
  * its list of video-game uses runs to about forty titles across a dozen
    platforms, from Warcraft II and UFO: Enemy Unknown to Ocarina of Time.

**Slayer is not on that list.** If the identification is right, this repository
holds a use of the cue that the catalogue of its uses does not -- which is a
pleasant thing to be able to say and is still not a measurement. Nothing in
docs/ depends on it.

7. MORE RE-USE, HEARD BEFORE IT WAS MEASURED
---------------------------------------------

The owner then reported, on the growls:

  * "Bulette.aiff e' IDENTICO a Ankheg.aiff come suono" -- and asked whether
    they were identical in bytes. **They are.** 36,608 bytes of sample data,
    equal byte for byte, sitting 256 bytes into Bulette's 37,120. The SHA-1
    census does not see it because Bulette carries 512 bytes more silence.
    That is measured in docs/09 and by tools/aiffreuse.py, and the question
    came from him first;

  * "Gargoyle.aiff e' lo stesso suono degli altri due, ma pitchato piu'
    acuto." **This session could not confirm it.** The envelope ranking puts
    Gargoyle with Ankheg at 0.68 and with Margoyle at 0.93, but the
    sample-level correlation of every one of those pairs is about 0.02, and no
    resampling ratio tried brought them together. Reported as unconfirmed,
    with the negative stated;

  * on /data/sounds/squeals/Bug hit.aiff: "PALESEMENTE lo stesso suono di
    quando i cinghiali di Warcraft 2 vengono colpiti/uccisi, semplicemente Bug
    hit ha un pitch superiore", and he labelled it himself: **"inferenza mia
    comunque"**, and that nearly all of these are probably stock.

THE STATUS OF THE THIRD ONE, AND IT IS THE INTERESTING ONE

It cannot be tested here: this session has no copy of Warcraft II and admits no
external audio. It is his inference, labelled by him as an inference, and this
repository publishes it as one and builds nothing on it.

What is worth writing down beside it is a convergence rather than a
confirmation. The Sound Ideas page he relayed in note 6 -- for the cue he heard
in Ankheg -- lists **Warcraft II: Tides of Darkness** among that cue's users.
If both of his identifications are right, this disc and Warcraft II drew on the
same commercial library two years apart. **That is a coincidence of two
unverified claims, not evidence, and it is written here because it is the sort
of thing a later session with a wider licence could actually check.**

8. AND THE ONE CONFIRMATION THAT WENT THE OTHER WAY
----------------------------------------------------

After tools/aiffreuse.py reported that Crypt Thing, Slithermorph and Yuan-ti
are one recording at three levels, the owner listened to all three:

  "Confermo, a livello auditivo non noto differenze tra quei 3 -- Slithermorph
   mi sembra un filo meno alto di volume."

-- he hears no difference between them except that Slithermorph seems a touch
quieter.

**The measurement, taken before he listened and without knowing what he would
say, puts Slithermorph at 5.66 dB below Crypt Thing and 6.09 dB below Yuan-ti,
and the other two within half a decibel of each other.** His ear and the least
squares fit picked the same file, in the same direction, on the only one of the
three that differs by more than a rounding error.

This is the only end-to-end check in the repository that runs from bytes to a
person and back. Everything else he reported, he reported first.
